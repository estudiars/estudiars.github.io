How To Take Out Flat Back Earrings: A Professional Guide To Threadless And Threaded Labrets

To remove a flat back earring safely, you must first identify whether the jewelry utilizes a threadless (push-pin) or a threaded mechanism. Successful removal requires stabilizing the flat-back post with one hand while applying either a firm outward pull for threadless styles or a counter-clockwise rotation for threaded styles, ensuring the use of nitrile gloves for maximum friction and grip.

Pre-Removal Sanitization and Equipment Checklist

Before attempting to remove professional-grade piercing jewelry, you must understand that flat back earrings (technically known as labret studs) are designed for long-term wear and security. Unlike "butterfly back" earrings found in department stores, these use precision-machined components that can be difficult to manipulate with bare fingers. The following preparation ensures you do not introduce bacteria into the piercing fistula or cause micro-tears in the tissue through excessive force.



Essential Gear and Pre-Procedure Standards



  • High-Grip Nitrile Gloves: These are the most critical tool for removal. Professional jewelry is often polished to a mirror finish, making it nearly impossible to grip with skin oils. Nitrile provides the necessary friction to torque or pull the decorative end.
  • Sterile Saline Solution (0.9% Sodium Chloride): Used to soften any "crusties" or dried lymph fluid that may have acted as a biological adhesive around the post.
  • Woven Gauze or Paper Towels: Used for drying the area and providing a secondary grip surface if gloves are unavailable.
  • Magnifying Mirror and High-Lumen Lighting: Essential for visualizing the junction between the decorative end and the post, especially for piercings in the cartilage like the tragus or helix.
  • Estimated Duration: 2 to 5 minutes.
  • Mandatory Prerequisite: Ensure the piercing is fully healed (typically 3–6 months for lobes and 6–12 months for cartilage) unless removal is necessitated by an emergency or jewelry upgrade by a professional.

Step-by-Step Removal Protocols for Flat Back Studs

The removal technique differs significantly based on the internal architecture of the jewelry. Most professional flat backs are either "Threadless" (relying on tension) or "Internally Threaded" (relying on a screw mechanism).



Step 1: Soften Biological Adhesion

Before touching the jewelry, inspect the back of the ear and the front of the piercing for any buildup of dried cellular debris (sebum and lymph). This buildup can effectively "glue" the jewelry in place.



  1. Saturate a piece of sterile gauze with saline solution.
  2. Hold the damp gauze against the front and back of the piercing for 60 seconds.
  3. Gently wipe away any softened debris.
  4. Thoroughly dry your hands and the ear area. Moisture reduces the friction needed for removal.


Step 2: Establish a Secure Anchor Point

The "flat back" or disk is the anchor of the jewelry. If the post spins freely while you try to remove the top, the mechanism will never disengage.



  1. Reach behind your ear and place your thumb or index finger firmly against the flat disk.
  2. Press the disk forward so the post protrudes slightly from the front of the ear. This creates space to grab the decorative end.
  3. If the piercing is in a tight area like the tragus, use the side of your finger to pin the disk against the ear's cartilage.


Step 3: Identify and Execute the Removal Movement

This is where the two main types of flat backs diverge in technique.

For Threadless (Push-Pin) Jewelry: Threadless jewelry consists of a decorative head with a small, slightly bent pin that slides into a hollow post. The bend creates the tension that keeps it in place.



  1. Grip the decorative front end firmly with your dominant hand (using gloves).
  2. While holding the back disk stationary, pull the front end straight out away from the post.
  3. You may need to use a slight "wiggling" motion to overcome the initial friction, but do not twist, as there are no threads to unscrew.

Pro-Tip: If the jewelry is brand new from a piercer, the "pre-bend" may be aggressive. Do not be afraid to apply significant steady pressure; the titanium is designed to withstand this force.

For Internally Threaded Jewelry: Threaded jewelry features a tiny screw on the decorative end that twists into the hollow, threaded post.



  1. Grip the decorative front end firmly.
  2. Apply the "Lefty-Loosey" rule. Looking in a mirror, you must rotate the front end to the left (counter-clockwise).
  3. Continue rotating until the decorative end completely detaches from the post.

Warning: Be extremely careful not to drop the decorative end. Since these are often made of precious metals or contain gemstones, they are small and easily lost in sink drains. Always plug the drain before beginning.



Step 4: Extract the Post

Once the decorative top is removed, the post is still inside the piercing.



  1. Gently push the post from the front to the back.
  2. Reach behind the ear and pull the flat disk until the post slides out of the fistula.
  3. If you feel any resistance or sharp pain, stop immediately. This indicates the piercing may not be fully healed or that there is a "flare" of tissue holding the jewelry.


Step 5: Post-Removal Hygiene



  1. Clean the removed jewelry with mild soap and warm water (or an ultrasonic cleaner if available).
  2. Wipe the piercing site with saline solution.
  3. If you are not replacing the jewelry immediately, be aware that piercing holes—especially in cartilage—can begin to shrink or close within minutes or hours, even in years-old piercings.

Troubleshooting Common Flat Back Removal Failures

Even with the correct technique, certain factors can make a flat back earring feel "stuck." Below are the most common failure scenarios and how to resolve them without visiting an emergency room.



Scenario 1: The "Spinning Post" Syndrome

Root Cause: You are attempting to unscrew or pull the top, but the entire jewelry unit (front and back) is spinning as one piece because the post is not being stabilized.



  • Actionable Fix: Use a pair of sterilized needle-nose pliers or professional hemostats to hold the post behind the ear (between the earlobe and the flat disk). Once the post is immobilized, use your other hand to unscrew or pull the top.


Scenario 2: Over-Tensioned Threadless Pin

Root Cause: The piercer may have put a significant "kink" in the threadless pin to ensure it never falls out. This creates a high level of resistance.



  • Actionable Fix: Increase your grip using a small piece of rubberized shelf liner or latex-free gloves. If pulling with your fingers fails, you can slide a piece of dental floss behind the decorative head, wrap it around your fingers, and pull outward. The floss provides a "handle" to apply more direct force.


Scenario 3: Vacuum/Protein Seal (The "Bio-Glue" Effect)

Root Cause: Skin cells and lymph have dried inside the hollow post or around the threads, creating a hermetic seal.



  • Actionable Fix: Take a hot shower for at least 10 minutes, allowing the steam and water to penetrate the jewelry. The heat expands the metal slightly and softens the organic matter. Attempt removal immediately after the shower while the tissue is most pliable.


Scenario 4: Cross-Threaded Top

Root Cause: The decorative end was screwed in at an angle, jamming the threads of the screw into the walls of the post.



  • Actionable Fix: Do not use more force, as this can snap the gold or titanium pin. Apply a drop of water-based lubricant or specialized jewelry oil to the junction. If it still won't budge, a professional piercer will need to use specialized tools (jewelry pliers) to gain the necessary leverage to break the cross-thread.

Frequently Asked Questions



Can I remove my flat back earring if the piercing is only two weeks old?

You should generally avoid removing or changing a piercing this early unless there is an allergic reaction to the metal. Removing jewelry from a fresh piercing causes the "tunnel" (fistula) to collapse, and you will likely be unable to reinsert the jewelry without professional help, increasing the risk of infection and trauma.



Which way do I turn a flat back earring to loosen it?

For threaded jewelry, you must turn the decorative front end to the left (counter-clockwise) while looking in a mirror. Because mirrors flip your perspective, it is helpful to think of it as moving the top of the jewelry toward your nose if the piercing is on your right ear, or toward your ear's outer edge if you are working on the opposite side.



Why is my flat back earring so hard to take out compared to a butterfly back?

Flat back labrets are "body-safe" jewelry designed for long-term wear, often used in "sleep-safe" setups. Unlike butterfly backs, which rely on a simple friction notch, professional labrets use precision engineering (threading or tension pins) to ensure they do not fall out during sleep, exercise, or showering.



Is it normal for my piercing to bleed when I take out the flat back?

If the piercing is fully healed, there should be no blood. If bleeding occurs, it indicates the piercing was either not fully healed or that the removal process was too aggressive, causing a tear in the fistula. Clean the area with saline and reinsert clean jewelry immediately to prevent the wound from closing.



How do I know if my flat back is threadless or threaded?

If you cannot remember what your piercer installed, try a gentle "twist" first. If the top spins indefinitely without coming off or feeling tighter/looser, it is likely threadless. If you feel resistance that increases as you turn one way and decreases the other, it is threaded.

Contact a Professional Piercer

If you encounter significant pain, swelling, or jewelry that refuses to budge after following these steps, seek assistance from an APP-certified professional piercer. They possess the specialized tools, such as taper pins and hemostats, required to remove or adjust high-tension jewelry without damaging your anatomy.
